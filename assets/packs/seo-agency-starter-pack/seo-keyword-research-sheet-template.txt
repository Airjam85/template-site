WORKBOOK TAB 1 — INSTRUCTIONS & SOP
========================================

PURPOSE

This workbook organizes keyword research from initial brainstorming through content mapping and implementation. It should serve as the shared source of truth for keyword selection, page targeting, content creation, and SEO prioritization.

RECOMMENDED WORKFLOW

Step 1: Review the client's products, services, audience, and business goals.

Step 2: Add initial topics, customer pain points, product categories, and seed keywords to the Seed Brainstorming tab.

Step 3: Expand the seed list using keyword research platforms, Google Search Console, Google Ads Keyword Planner, autocomplete suggestions, related searches, People Also Ask results, customer questions, and competitor research.

Step 4: Import all relevant terms into the Master Keyword Database.

Step 5: Remove exact duplicates, irrelevant terms, and keywords outside the client's target market.

Step 6: Assign search intent, funnel stage, business priority, keyword role, and topic cluster.

Step 7: Review current rankings and identify new-content opportunities, optimization opportunities, competitor gaps, and possible keyword cannibalization.

Step 8: Assign each approved keyword cluster to one target URL or planned page.

Step 9: Move actionable opportunities into the Content Map or Quick Wins tab.

Step 10: Review rankings and implementation status regularly.

SEARCH INTENT DEFINITIONS

Informational:
The searcher wants information, an explanation, instructions, or an answer to a question.

Examples:
- What is local SEO?
- How to conduct keyword research
- Benefits of content marketing

Commercial:
The searcher is comparing products, services, providers, or possible solutions before making a decision.

Examples:
- Best SEO agency for small businesses
- Semrush vs. Ahrefs
- Top payroll software

Transactional:
The searcher is ready to buy, book, subscribe, request a quote, download, or complete another conversion action.

Examples:
- Hire an SEO consultant
- Buy accounting software
- Book emergency plumber

Navigational:
The searcher wants to reach a particular website, brand, product, or online destination.

Examples:
- Google Search Console login
- Semrush pricing
- [Brand Name] customer support

FUNNEL-STAGE DEFINITIONS

Top of Funnel:
Awareness-focused terms used by people learning about a problem or topic.

Middle of Funnel:
Evaluation-focused terms used by people researching possible solutions.

Bottom of Funnel:
High-intent terms used by people close to making a purchase or contacting a provider.

KEYWORD ROLE DEFINITIONS

Primary Keyword:
The main search term assigned to a page. Each page should generally have one primary keyword.

Secondary Keyword:
A closely related phrase, variation, question, or subtopic that supports the primary keyword and may be targeted on the same page.

Supporting Keyword:
A related term that may inform headings, FAQs, examples, internal links, or supplemental content.

BUSINESS PRIORITY DEFINITIONS

High:
Closely aligned with a primary product, service, revenue source, or strategic business objective.

Medium:
Relevant to the business and useful for attracting or educating potential customers but less directly tied to immediate revenue.

Low:
Topically relevant but less likely to influence qualified traffic, conversions, or current campaign priorities.

DATA-ENTRY RULES

- Use one keyword per row.
- Use lowercase unless capitalization is required for a brand or proper noun.
- Do not combine multiple keywords in one cell.
- Record the source and date of every metric export.
- Use the same country, language, database, and device settings when comparing keyword metrics.
- Do not use search volume as the only prioritization factor.
- Review actual search results before assigning intent or content type.
- Map one keyword cluster to one preferred page whenever possible.
- Flag multiple URLs targeting the same primary keyword as a possible cannibalization issue.
- Never overwrite historical ranking data without preserving the prior measurement date.

RECOMMENDED COLUMN GUIDANCE

Keyword:
Enter the exact query being evaluated.

Normalized Keyword:
Enter a cleaned version used for deduplication. Remove unnecessary capitalization, punctuation, and spacing without changing meaning.

Topic Cluster:
Group keywords that should be addressed by the same page or tightly related page set.

Keyword Role:
Mark the main term as Primary and related variations as Secondary or Supporting.

Search Intent:
Classify the dominant intent based on the current search results, not solely on the wording of the query.

Business Priority:
Evaluate how closely the keyword aligns with profitable products, services, customer segments, or campaign goals.

Relevance Score:
Use a consistent scale such as 1 to 5, where 5 means directly aligned with the client's core offering.

Search Volume:
Enter average monthly searches for the selected country and language.

Keyword Difficulty:
Enter the difficulty metric from the chosen research platform. Do not compare difficulty scores from different tools as though they use the same calculation.

Cost Per Click:
Enter the estimated average advertising cost per click. A higher CPC may indicate commercial value but should not be treated as a direct SEO ranking factor.

Traffic Potential:
Estimate the possible traffic from the entire topic rather than only the exact keyword where data is available.

Search Trend:
Record Growing, Stable, Declining, Seasonal, Emerging, or Unknown.

SERP Features:
Record applicable features such as Featured Snippet, People Also Ask, Local Pack, Images, Video, Shopping, Reviews, AI Overview, or Knowledge Panel.

Current Ranking Position:
Enter the current organic position for the selected device and location. Use Not Ranking when no position is found within the tracked range.

Position Change:
Use a formula comparing the current and previous ranking positions.

Example Formula:
==IF(OR(X2="",Y2=""),"",Y2-X2)

Assigned URL:
Enter the preferred existing or planned page that should rank for the keyword cluster.

Proposed URL Slug:
Enter a concise, descriptive proposed path for new content.

Recommended Action:
Choose the most appropriate implementation step based on current rankings, content quality, intent alignment, and business value.

Cannibalization Risk:
Mark Yes when multiple URLs compete for the same or nearly identical intent. Mark Possible when further review is needed.

Opportunity Score:
Use a consistent formula based on business priority, relevance, search demand, difficulty, intent, and current position.

SAMPLE OPPORTUNITY SCORE MODEL

Assign numeric values:

Business Priority:
High = 3
Medium = 2
Low = 1

Relevance Score:
1 to 5

Intent Value:
Transactional = 4
Commercial = 3
Informational = 2
Navigational = 1

Ranking Opportunity:
Positions 4–10 = 4
Positions 11–20 = 5
Positions 21–30 = 4
Positions 31–50 = 3
Not Ranking = 2
Positions 1–3 = 1

Difficulty Opportunity:
KD 0–20 = 5
KD 21–40 = 4
KD 41–60 = 3
KD 61–80 = 2
KD 81–100 = 1

Example Weighted Formula:

Opportunity Score =
(Business Priority × 25%) +
(Relevance × 20%) +
(Intent Value × 20%) +
(Ranking Opportunity × 20%) +
(Difficulty Opportunity × 15%)

Adjust weights according to the campaign's goals and available authority.

CONTENT MAPPING RULES

- Assign one primary keyword to each page.
- Add approximately three to five closely related secondary keywords where appropriate.
- Do not force unrelated intents onto the same page.
- Separate informational and transactional pages when the search results show clearly different expectations.
- Use pillar pages for broad topics that require multiple supporting articles.
- Map location-specific transactional terms to unique, useful location pages where appropriate.
- Use existing pages when they already match the search intent and can be improved.
- Create new content only when there is a genuine gap or the existing page cannot satisfy the query.
- Consolidate overlapping pages where multiple URLs address the same intent.

COMPETITOR GAP REVIEW QUESTIONS

- Is the keyword genuinely relevant to the client's products, services, or audience?
- Does the ranking content match the client's intended search market?
- Are competitors ranking with a service page, product page, guide, comparison, video, or another format?
- Does the client already have a page that could compete after optimization?
- Would creating this page introduce cannibalization?
- Does the keyword have enough business value to justify the required effort?
- What does the ranking competitor content include that the client currently lacks?

CLUSTERING GUIDELINES

- Group terms only when the same page can satisfy their search intent.
- Review the current search results to determine whether Google treats keywords as equivalent.
- Use the highest-value and most representative term as the primary keyword, not automatically the term with the highest volume.
- Keep keywords with substantially different commercial intent in separate clusters.
- Keep location-specific terms separate when unique local pages are justified.
- Use secondary keywords naturally in headings, body content, examples, and FAQs.
